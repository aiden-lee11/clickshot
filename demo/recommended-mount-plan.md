# Recommended hybrid — class-build mock

This is the fourth mount option under **Mount builds**: Original printed, Plywood frame, Compact inverted and Recommended hybrid. Use the build selector or previous/next arrows. The direct link ends in `#recommended`.

## Construction

Use a trimmed receiver, two short flat plywood uprights and a rectangular plywood yaw deck. Ordinary angle brackets secure the uprights. Small printed housings hold bought pitch bearings; two separate short pivot bolts support the cradle. Print only the saddles, light cradle rails, bearing housings and servo/horn interfaces. Supplied servo horns provide spline engagement.

A bought turntable rests on four 60 mm wood risers. A fixed coaxial yaw servo fits below it and turns the deck through a horn adapter in the bearing's center opening. This trades some height/wood for simpler yaw assembly. There is **no yaw crank or pushrod**. One outboard servo and short crank/pushrod drive pitch, around the side of the plywood upright.

The battery and controller sit on the fixed, clamped plywood base. The camera/rangefinder remain upright on the moving cradle. Two padded saddles/straps retain the receiver and leave an opening between them for the jam door. Retain shell structure around the feed system, magazine latch, pusher, flywheels and mounting points.

These dimensions illustrate packaging. Transfer-drill the actual turntable pattern; its bearing-circle diameter is not its mounting-hole pitch. Measure servo tabs, body/shaft datums, horn thickness/hole pattern and pivot stacks before fabrication. The drawn carrier, adapters and bearing housings have not been converted to new fabrication CAD. Real screw heads, nuts, tools, cable slack and linkage joints require clearance checks.

The animated pitch rod uses 100 mm pin-to-pin spacing and a 24 mm crank radius. Its mathematical closure is shown, not a selected linkage kit or calibrated servo mapping. Yaw is coaxial, without a rod. Linkages hide in the exploded view because it is not an operating pose.

## Orientation and balance

The default mock is inverted so the magazine is above the receiver. **Confirm reliable inverted feeding, pusher behavior, magazine retention and jam-door access before selecting this orientation.** Inversion reduces underside clearance; it does not guarantee a lower center of mass. Find the loaded balance point after trimming, including the magazine, camera and attached wiring, then set saddle/pivot placement and frame height. Balance changes as darts are used. The balance symbol in the scene is a proposed pivot marker, not a measured center of mass.

If inverted operation is unreliable, revise the upright height and clearances for an upright receiver. Flat plywood pieces make that revision straightforward. Do not cut the mount to the mock dimensions before taking those measurements.
